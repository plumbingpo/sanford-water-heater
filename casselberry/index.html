How Casselberry Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Most early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like rusty hot water, rumbling during heating, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away the moment something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.
