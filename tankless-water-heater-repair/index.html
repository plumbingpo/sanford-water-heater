A tankless water heater keeps no reserve of hot water. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is efficient and space-saving, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In Sanford, FL, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

Sanford Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in Sanford, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat gives itself away in those numbers. Error codes narrow the search, but they rarely name the broken part outright. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

The good news is that most tankless problems are very fixable. Scale can be flushed out, sensors and igniters are replaceable parts, and venting faults usually come down to a blocked intake or a loose joint. We are also honest about the harder cases: a unit fed by a gas line too small for its full BTU rating will keep throwing codes until the line is upsized, and a heat exchanger damaged by scale may not be worth repairing on an older heater. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • The unit clicking or trying to light several times before giving up
  • Small draws, like a bathroom sink, that never trigger the heater
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Unusual noises from the cabinet, like a whine, a roar, or a rattle
  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before

Why Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair Matters

Tankless repair is a poor fit for DIY for two reasons: the diagnosis is tricky and the hazards are real. On the diagnostic side, an ignition code might mean a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and replacing parts until the code clears adds up fast. On the safety side, a gas unit burns fuel at a very high rate and vents exhaust that contains carbon monoxide, while an electric unit can pull more than 100 amps through multiple 240-volt breakers. Errors with either type can be serious.

What you get from a professional is certainty. Instead of swapping parts, our technicians confirm the fault with a meter, a manometer, and temperature readings, so the fix is aimed at the actual cause. Descaling is done with a pump and service valves, not by pouring vinegar into an inlet and hoping. Gas pressure is checked with the burner at full output, vent runs are inspected against the manufacturer's length and clearance limits, and the unit is tested at low and high flow before we leave. Proper service also keeps you in line with the manufacturer's warranty terms, many of which expect qualified service and regular descaling.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Treat a code that returns after a reset as a symptom worth diagnosing, not ignoring
  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
  • Flush scale out of the heat exchanger annually, and sooner in hard water
  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
