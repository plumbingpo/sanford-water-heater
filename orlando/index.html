Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Orlando

Most early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular service, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Orlando Homeowners Face

A water heater in Orlando rarely fails out of nowhere. Look out for rumbling and popping during heating, brownish hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. These are early signs of heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and when they're caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Ignored, they lead to a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
